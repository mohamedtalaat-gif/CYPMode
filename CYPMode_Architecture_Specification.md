# CYPMode: Architecture Specification (v1)

This document is the reference for the architecture of CYPMode. It covers the time-dependent inhibition (TDI) track, which was the original scope, and the structure prediction track, which entered the project on 2026-09-25 (see the section "Official challenge update"). The two tracks form one project. The inputs and outputs of each track are kept in separate folders: `data/tdi_track/outputs/` for TDI, and `data/structure_track/inputs/` and `data/structure_track/outputs/cyp3a4/structure_track_results.json` for structure prediction. The document supersedes the diagrams that were sketched during planning. The conclusions on which each TDI-track decision rests are stated here; the numbers are in the audit report (`audit/AUDIT_REPORT.md`, sections 4 and 9).

## Final architecture

This section describes the model that is submitted. The sections after it are the record of how the project arrived there: those up to the decision log describe the first architecture of 2026-09-27 and the tests that followed, and the section "Update: nested evaluation, gated additions and the assembled model" lists what was added since. Where an older section conflicts with this one, this one holds. The tables of the final stack and of the structure recipe are generated from the final build and stand at the end of the document; the same description, with every number read from the manifests, is in sections 3 and 6 of `METHOD_REPORT.md`.

**TDI classification (CYP3A4 and CYP2D6).**

1. *Label and population.* A compound is positive when `s = t - max(d, 4) > log10 2`, with `t` the pIC50 of the TDI arm and `d` that of the direct-inhibition arm; the rule reproduces every training label. The scored population is the one with both arms measured, and only confidently labelled compounds (`z = |s - log10 2| / sd(s)`) are scored; models are judged on that population, weighted by similarity to the blinded structures and by the estimated prevalence of the scored set.
2. *Features.* Label-free blocks: Morgan counts and RDKit descriptors, MACCS, Avalon and ErG fingerprints, the Type II motif flag and structural alerts, GFN2-xTB physics at several levels, and frozen embeddings of pretrained networks as opt-in blocks. No block reads a label of a test compound.
3. *The default stack.* For each isoform, a set of diverse candidates (boosted trees on several feature sets, forests, Tanimoto k-nearest-neighbour and SVM models, regressors of the two assay arms) is blended by a logistic regression on the ranks of the members, with the penalty chosen on the inner folds, and calibrated by isotonic regression.
4. *Groups added through a gate.* Every further idea is a candidate group that enters a stack only when its pre-registered gate is met alone and inside the stack (corrected resampled t below 0.05 at both confidence cut-offs, and a replicate on ten outer folds for a group found after looking). For CYP3A4 two groups joined: the neural group (a multitask network over the assay arms and plain Chemprop) and the Chemprop group started from two pretrained OpenADMET models and fine-tuned. For CYP2D6 no group passed, and the final stack is the default stack.
5. *Calls.* The positive calls are the top share of the test compounds given by a multiple of the estimated prevalence of the scored population, the multiple being chosen on the nested folds.
6. *CYP2D6 has two files.* The hits-only file and the selection-aware file, which multiplies P(label | hit) by a gate P(hit); a rule written before any upload chooses the entry (the hits-only file) and the other file is kept to be scored on the test labels if the organisers publish them.

**Structure prediction (CYP3A4, 20 ligands).** Boltz-2 co-folding with the heme and its covalent bond to the proximal cysteine; a restraint of the iron-nitrogen contact for a ligand with a Type II donor (its gate is met); a pool of samples per ligand; the scorer's own PoseBusters call on every pose, with eligibility by a margin rule; the model's own ranking among the eligible poses (every rule for choosing a pose failed its gate); a force-field relaxation of the chosen poses under a rule registered before its use; a check that every pose carries the official stereochemistry; a deterministic archive.

**What is not in the final model.** The ideas that did not pass their gates (the direct-inhibition bridge, the structural features of Tier 3, the quantum-electronic and pharmacophore-fingerprint features, ChemBERTa embeddings, the shared-trunk network, the pocket and site blocks, a committee of models for uncertainty, and the rest) are recorded with their numbers in `NEGATIVE_RESULTS.md` where a gate or a paired comparison judged them and in the sections below for the earlier ones (the bridge, Tier 3, the shared trunk, the committee), and are taught in the notebook with the reasons that they added nothing.

## Official challenge update (2026-09-25, halfway-point blog post)

OpenADMET published the following in the post "Halfway Through the OpenADMET CYP Challenge" and on the challenge Discord. The information bears on how the results of the project are interpreted and prioritized.

- **Interim leaderboard on the full test set** (not the half-set live leaderboard). A Benjamini-Hochberg procedure groups statistically indistinguishable submissions into tiers. This agrees with the move of the project toward Tukey HSD-corrected significance testing (see the section on combined-feature stacking below).
- **TDI track: 26 teams were statistically tied in Tier 1**, with MCC from 0.341 (aries) to 0.398 (nova). The practical target is therefore MCC of about 0.34 or more, not a rank among the points below it. The best validated single-isoform numbers of the project at that date (CYP3A4 about 0.375 tuned, CYP2D6 about 0.09) left a gap to close, mainly for CYP2D6.
- **Explanation for CYP2D6 being the harder isoform, in addition to the earlier finding on assay noise.** The hit expansion that built the test set prioritized the top hits of CYP3A4, CYP1A2 and CYP2C9, not those of CYP2D6, so the CYP2D6 test compounds are less potent than those of the other three isoforms. This is a train/test distribution shift specific to CYP2D6, in addition to measurement noise.
- **A Structure Track was launched on that day** and runs alongside TDI and Direct Inhibition through 3 November 2026. It consists of 20 cryo-EM CYP3A4 structures (15 from the training set, 2 from the test set, 3 additional), scored primarily by LDDT-PLI (secondary: BiSyRMSD) through the OpenStructure pipeline. **PoseBusters is part of the scoring**: a submitted structure that fails the physical and chemical checks of PoseBusters scores LDDT-PLI = 0 whatever its geometric accuracy. The PoseBusters installation of the project was therefore raised from a validity gate to a priority.

**Local batch and official set.** The local batch was compared with the official set by cross-referencing `data/structure_track/inputs/structure_track_test_blinded.csv` (the official 20-compound set) with every prediction directory in `data/structure_track/outputs/out/` (78 in total, from four sources: the 20 official compounds; 50 separate Tier 3 compounds that were selected for TDI feature engineering by out-of-fold uncertainty, a different population of which only 1 compound overlaps; the 4-compound TDI reference panel; and 5 other named reference drugs). All 20 official Structure Track compounds are present, so no reconciliation was needed.

**PoseBusters batch result.** PoseBusters was run on every completed structure (`cypmode.validation.pose_validity`). The count below is that of the last run before the corrections described in the next paragraph; it changes as more structures complete, and `docs/figures/structure_track/` draws that first batch. **46 of 57 structures (80.7%) pass every check.** Ten fail the same check, `minimum_distance_to_protein`: a steric clash, not a borderline rounding case (for itraconazole the smallest ligand-protein distance is 2.42 Å, 74.4% of the safe van der Waals sum threshold, with one clashing atom pair). At that date the clashes had not been remedied, for example by local clash-relief minimization or by re-prediction with other settings. The result confirms the failure mode that the blog post of OpenADMET describes ("co-folding algorithms often optimize for raw atom placement at the expense of physical plausibility") and was found locally, before any submission.

The eleventh failure, the RDKit valence-parsing error for `OCNT-2311782` during ligand reconstruction, was diagnosed and fixed in the code audit of 2026-09-26. The SMILES of the compound was valid. The error came from proximity-based bond perception, which read two atoms that are close but not bonded in the predicted conformation as bonded (29 perceived bonds against 27 in the molecule); `rdDetermineBonds.DetermineConnectivity` of RDKit made the same error, so the choice of tool was not the cause. The fix is a verified fallback in `cypmode.validation.structures._ligand_mol_3d` that avoids distance-based bond perception whenever the atom-order correspondence is confirmed and every template bond length is physically reasonable, and that falls back further otherwise. The file `posebusters_validity.csv` needed a new run to include this compound and the complete 20/20 Structure Track set.

## Official challenge rules and scoring detail (from the HF Space FAQ of OpenADMET)

The FAQ gives the following rules and scoring mechanics.

- **Isoform panel.** Direct Inhibition (regression) is scored across four isoforms: CYP3A4, CYP2C9, CYP2D6 and CYP1A2. TDI, the track of this project, is scored on CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 only.
- **Two independent tracks and leaderboards.** Direct Inhibition (regression) and TDI (classification) are submitted as separate files and ranked independently; a team can enter either or both. The FAQ does not mention the Structure Track, which was launched later. It is scored on LDDT-PLI, BiSyRMSD and PoseBusters, metrics that cannot be merged with MCC or MA-ST-RAE into one number, so it is probably ranked independently too; this is not confirmed in writing.
- **Test set.** The test set has 750 compounds and is released in a single continuous stage (not in two sequential unblinding phases, as in some earlier OpenADMET challenges). An interim leaderboard is published at the halfway mark, and the full ground truth is withheld until the challenge closes.
- **Primary metric of Direct Inhibition.** The metric is the Macro-Averaged Soft-Threshold Relative Absolute Error (MA-ST-RAE). A prediction inside the credible interval of the fitted dose-response curve incurs no penalty, so that models are not penalized for missing noisy point estimates of low-activity compounds (pIC50 below 4, under the reliable dynamic range of the assay). The metric is outside the TDI-only scope of the project and is recorded as context for how the organizers treat noisy assay regions.
- **TDI labelling rule** (the training data carry it as the pre-computed `{cyp}_is_TDI` columns, so the FAQ confirms what is used). A compound is **positive** if its direct-inhibition pIC50 is above 4 (the reliable floor of the assay) and the shift of the TDI arm exceeds 2-fold (log10(2) = 0.301). It is **negative** if the pIC50 is above 4 and the shift does not clear 2-fold. It is **inferred positive** if the direct-inhibition pIC50 is below 4 (too low to measure a shift directly) and the pIC50 of the TDI arm itself exceeds 4.301, which guarantees a shift above 2-fold. It is **assigned negative** if both arms are below 4: TDI cannot be determined, and compounds this inactive in both arms are labelled negative by convention, because they would not be prioritized for kinetic follow-up. The scored positive class is positive plus inferred positive; the scored negative class is negative plus assigned negative.
- **Proprietary data.** Submitters tick a box that states the use of proprietary data, and it is shown on the leaderboard. CYPMode uses only public data (PubChem, BindingDB, TDC), so the box stays unticked.
- **Open source.** Releasing the code is encouraged but not required for the standard ranking; it is required for the separate "Innovation in ML Award" (architectural novelty, creative data usage, novel uncertainty quantification, clever engineering, independent of leaderboard rank). The statistical-significance discipline of the project, its use of external data and its record of negative results are the kind of work that this award names.
- **Rate and deadline.** Submissions are limited to one per 12 hours, and only the latest valid submission counts. The final deadline is **3 November 2026, 23:59 UTC**, the same as that of the Structure Track.
- **No summary preprint.** OpenADMET publishes blog posts and Zenodo technical reports instead, which is the citation pattern used below for the ExpansionRx retrospective.

## Lessons from the OpenADMET-ExpansionRx blind challenge (Castellanos et al. 2026)

A separate, completed OpenADMET community blind challenge was written up by the organizing team of the CYP challenge, with Pat Walters as corresponding author. It covered nine ADMET endpoints (LogD, kinetic solubility, microsomal clearance, Caco-2 permeability, protein and tissue binding) on a proprietary dataset of 7,608 compounds from Expansion Therapeutics, from September 2025 to January 2026. It is not the CYP challenge, but several of its findings bear on the methodology of the project and are recorded, positive and negative alike.

- **Statistical methodology.** The organizers found and corrected a pseudoreplication error in their first significance testing. Tukey HSD applied directly to bootstrap-resampled metric distributions inflates the apparent significance, because bootstrap resampling of a fixed set of precomputed predictions is not the same as the independently retrained replicates (for example 5×5 nested cross-validation with different seeds and folds) that Tukey or ANOVA assumes; treating B bootstrap iterations as independent samples drives MS_Error/n toward zero and collapses the p-value. The correction, paired bootstrap-difference distributions with Holm-Bonferroni or Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment, is stated to apply to "current and future challenges, such as the OpenADMET PXR and CYP blind challenges", and is the methodology behind the Benjamini-Hochberg tiering of the CYP challenge (see above). On 2026-09-27 the approach of `cypmode.challenge.stacking_significance` was judged to lie on the correct side of this debate, because it used repeated 5×5 cross-validation with independent retraining per fold and seed (the protocol of Ash et al. that the paper cites as valid) and not bootstrap resampling of a fixed prediction set. That judgment was revised later: the Tukey HSD of that module treated the 25 correlated folds as independent groups, and the paired corrected resampled t replaced it (see the update at the end of this document).
- **Ceiling of classical machine learning.** Among the top 20 ExpansionRx entries, 2D graph neural networks (mostly Chemprop) and chemical foundation-model pretraining (CheMeleon) dominated, and models that rely on classical machine learning alone (LightGBM, XGBoost or CatBoost on descriptors and fingerprints, the approach of CYPMode at that date) ranked no higher than 46th. The task differs from CYP TDI classification (binary classification on about 6,100 compounds against 9 continuous endpoints on about 7,600 compounds from a denser optimization-campaign series), so the result is a signal and not a proof. It matters most if the MCC of CYPMode plateaus well below Tier 1 after the current feature-engineering avenues are exhausted.
- **Two choices that the paper supports.** (1) External public data was strongly associated with top-tier performance (16 of the top 20 entries used it), which is the direction of the PubChem and BindingDB auxiliary-feature work of the project. (2) Hyperparameter optimization was not a major driver for the top teams ("data engineering and feature augmentation mattered more than architecture-level tuning"), consistent with where the effort of the project has gone.
- **A generic blind spot.** Both the graph neural network or foundation-model approaches and the classical fingerprint approaches failed to predict property differences between stereoisomers. The predicted differences collapsed to near zero even where the measured differences were large (a median predicted difference of about 0.005 to 0.007 log units for enantiomers whose measured median difference was 0.056, and worse against the larger measured differences of diastereomers). Two-dimensional and topological features, and most 3D representations, carry little or no stereochemical signal by construction. The Morgan-fingerprint and RDKit-descriptor stack of CYPMode almost certainly shares this blind spot, and no fix is proposed here; it is recorded for the reading of any prediction on a stereoisomer pair.
- **Applicability domain.** The paper states that knowing when a model applies is still harder than making it accurate: no applicability-domain signal that was tried, including a trained error model, exceeded a Spearman ρ of about 0.6 with the measured error. The OOD and applicability-domain score of `cypmode/challenge/reliability.py` is accordingly a useful signal and not a precise reliability guarantee.

Citation: Castellanos, M. A. et al. *Assessing the state of the art in ADMET prediction: Lessons from the OpenADMET-ExpansionRx community blind challenge.* ChemRxiv, 2026-09-23. https://doi.org/10.26434/chemrxiv.15009342.v1 (preprint, not yet peer-reviewed, CC BY 4.0).

## Evidence on Boltz-2 and OpenFold3 ligand-placement accuracy for CYP3A4 (Huddleston, OpenADMET blog, 2026-06-02)

The OpenADMET blog post "Cofolding? I hardly know her! - Cofolding Methods on ADMET Targets" (Part 1 of a series, DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21785463) evaluated **Boltz-2 and OpenFold3**, the two structure-prediction tools of the project, against PDB structures of CYP3A4 and PXR. The oracle-ranking protocol was used (the best of 5 samples per structure, selected against the ground truth), which is an optimistic upper bound and not what a blind submission can do. The result bears directly on the Structure Track work of the project.

- **Ligand placement succeeds (BiSyRMSD below 2 Å) in only 57.8% of the cases for Boltz-2 on CYP3A4 and 31.5% for OpenFold3**, although the backbone (Cα) RMSD stays under 0.75 Å in every case. A correct protein fold is not sufficient for a correct ligand pose. PXR was similar or worse (38.5% and 32.7%). A well-studied control target, p38 kinase, scored high on all metrics for both tools, which shows that the CYP3A4 and PXR failures are target-specific gaps and not a general malfunction of the tools.
- **The failure is not only a matter of training-data coverage.** 78.9% of the tested CYP3A4 structures were already in the training set of Boltz-2 (cutoff June 2023), and the performance was statistically the same whether a structure was in the training set or held out. The models do not learn the CYP3A4 ligand chemistry even from structures they were trained on.
- **A specific CYP3A4 failure mode.** The heme is anchored correctly essentially every time, but the orientation of the ligand relative to the heme is frequently wrong, and the most extreme failures (BiSyRMSD above 10 Å) are consistently allosteric-site ligands (for example progesterone) that are placed in the orthosteric, heme-proximal pocket. A known or suspected allosteric binder among the Structure Track compounds is therefore a named failure mode to check for.
- **Confidence-based model selection.** The post states that "the confidence and ranking scores output by these methods offer no useful signal... show no meaningful correlation with BiSyRMSD, and rarely rank the best predicted structure first." This is a caveat for `cypmode.validation.pose_validity.select_valid_model`, which tries `model_0`, `model_1`, ... in the confidence-ranked order of Boltz-2 until one passes PoseBusters. Passing PoseBusters (physical and chemical plausibility) is a different and useful bar from being geometrically correct against a true structure, and confidence ranking does not reliably surface the correct sample even when one exists among the 5. A structure that passes PoseBusters is not thereby confirmed accurate, and the PoseBusters pass rate of 80.7% at the last run is not the share of correct poses.
- **BiSyRMSD definition.** The stated method of the post ("Ligand RMSD after binding site superposition, corrected for molecular symmetry") is the Kabsch-pocket-superposition-then-symmetry-aware-RMSD approach that `cypmode.validation.bisy_rmsd` implements, which was arrived at before this post was found.

**Implication.** Boltz-2, which the project already uses, is the better of the two open options for CYP3A4 (57.8% against 31.5% for OpenFold3). That supports the existing choice of pipeline and is not a reason to switch to OpenFold3 as a replacement; the likely value of OpenFold3-preview here is a second, independent cross-check of a prediction, since the errors of the two models are not obviously of the same kind. Because the confidence scores carry no signal, the PoseBusters gate remains the right check to keep running, since it catches physically impossible structures, but it is not a proxy for pose correctness, which cannot be checked locally without a reference structure.

Citation: Huddleston, K. "Cofolding? I hardly know her! - Cofolding Methods on ADMET Targets." *OpenADMET Blog*, 2026-06-02. https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21785463

## Component-by-component

**Tier 1, universal chemical representation.** Morgan fingerprints (ECFP4, radius 2, 2048 bits) and RDKit descriptors, computed for every compound of the training and test sets, with no missing values.

**Tier 2, CYPMode mechanistic evidence.** The motif flag only (`cypmode.metrics.motifs`). The trialkylamine flag (`cypmode.metrics.amines`) was tested and dropped: it lowered the CYP2D6 MCC and did nothing for CYP3A4 in a multivariate model (the M0-M3 ablation). The chemistry behind it (the trialkylamine-mediated TDI of verapamil, diltiazem and troleandomycin) stands; what failed at scale is the specific feature engineering, a standalone binary flag.

**Correction (2026-10-16).** The evidence given above for dropping the trialkylamine flag does not hold. It rests on one five-fold partition (CYP2D6 MCC 0.069 → 0.038, corrected p = 0.31). Re-run with the pattern as it is today, the same step on that partition gives +0.040 (p = 0.51), and over five partitions (25 folds) the change in MCC from the motif-only to the motif-plus-amine model is +0.004 (corrected p = 0.88), with the partitions ranging from -0.040 to +0.040. The data neither support nor refute the flag. The decision to leave it out stands, since the flag (the `amine` block of the feature store) is not an input of any member of the final stack; the reason given for it does not.

**Tier 3, structural augmentation, CYP3A4 only.** Boltz-2 with an explicit heme and a covalent axial-cysteine bond (`cypmode.validation.pipeline`) was run on a **fixed budget of 50 compounds** (`cypmode.challenge.tier3_selection.select_fixed_budget_oof`), ranked by out-of-fold classification uncertainty across the whole labeled population. No CYP2D6 counterpart was built, deliberately: the project has no validated CYP2D6 structural pipeline, and constructing one only to obtain architectural symmetry would be a research project of its own. The CYP2D6 head therefore has no structural branch.

**Selection mask, revised from the original "motif OR uncertain" specification.** The data were checked first: motif-positive compounds alone cover about 42% of the labeled CYP3A4 population, so the original OR clause selected 46 to 72% of about 3,584 compounds whatever the quantile, which means thousands of compounds against an observed Boltz-2 cost of 15 to 30 minutes or more each, and is not tractable. The motif is already a free Tier 2 feature, so Boltz-2 time spent on a motif-positive compound that the model already knows adds nothing. The revised policy is a pure OOF-uncertainty ranking with no motif clause and a fixed budget (N=50) sized to the compute budget, in place of an open-ended fraction of the dataset. The threshold and ranking model is trained fold-internally and never sees the fold that it scores (`cypmode/challenge/tier3_selection.py` and its leakage tests).

**OOF Direct-Inhibition Bridge.** A separate regression model (`cypmode.challenge.oof_bridge`) predicts the direct-inhibition pIC50 with out-of-fold predictions (trained on the training fold and applied to the held-out fold), so that the validation labels of the TDI model do not leak through potency information. It was **revised from the original Tobit (censored-regression) plan**: the data were checked first, and no hard censoring at the pIC50 floor of 4 was found (the values continue smoothly down to about 1.9 with no pile-up). What is present is heteroscedastic measurement noise, with the reported standard deviation 5 to 10 times larger below pIC50 = 4. A Tobit model assumes the wrong generating process for these data, so an inverse-variance weighted regression (using the measured standard deviation of each compound) is used instead. The bridge feeds Feature/Evidence Fusion as a parallel input stream next to Tiers 1 to 3 and not as a step applied after them. Result: OOF MAE 0.730 for CYP3A4 and 0.645 for CYP2D6 (the pIC50 spans about 6 log units).

**Shared Trunk.** The shared trunk was treated as a hypothesis to test and not as an assumed design. A small shared-trunk MLP in plain PyTorch (`cypmode.challenge.multitask`: one trunk, one head per isoform, and a masked binary cross-entropy loss so that a compound that lacks the label of one isoform contributes no gradient to that head) was built in place of installing `openadmet-models`, whose dependencies (PyTorch, Lightning, ChemProp and CheMeleon, CatBoost, XGBoost, TabPFN) exist only in a separate conda environment without a pip-installable `pyproject.toml`.

**Comparison with the same 5-fold CV protocol and the same feature set (descriptors, motif and Morgan) throughout.** The comparison was run again on 2026-09-26 after a standardization leak was fixed in `cross_validate_shared_trunk` and `cross_validate_multi_branch_shared_trunk` (the mean and standard deviation of the full dataset had been computed before the fold loop, and not on the training fold only; code audit of 2026-09-26, item 4). Threshold tuning helps both architectures, and helps the independent-head model more (CYP3A4 +0.075, CYP2D6 +0.035) than the shared trunk (CYP3A4 +0.028, CYP2D6 +0.014); the gap between the two architectures narrows slightly for CYP3A4 and never closes. **The split is consistent and is not fold noise from a single run**: the independent heads are ahead for CYP3A4 in every variant tested, and the shared trunk is ahead for CYP2D6 in every variant tested. Neither architecture dominates the other.

**Reproducibility.** An earlier pass attributed a discrepancy between two shared-trunk result files (0.099 and 0.111 for the same CYP2D6 measurement) to PyTorch non-determinism. That attribution was wrong, and it was re-checked. `train_shared_trunk` was confirmed to be bit-for-bit deterministic on a controlled synthetic test at the exact scale of the data (3,800 rows by 2,267 features), and the discrepancy disappeared once `cross_validate_shared_trunk` was run again with the standardization-leak fix in place: the CYP2D6 naive-threshold values of both files now match exactly (0.0994). Running the entire `python -m cypmode.challenge.multitask` pipeline twice end to end produced byte-for-byte identical output files. The model is reproducible; the earlier concern came from a stale file from before the fix being compared with a file from after it.

**Decision: a per-isoform model choice for the final assembled model, not one global architecture.** CYP3A4 uses the tuned independent-head model (0.375) and CYP2D6 uses the tuned shared trunk (0.113). The asymmetry rests on the evidence above: the weak baseline signal of CYP2D6 (MCC of about 0.05 from the first baseline on) benefits from whatever cross-isoform regularization the shared trunk provides, while CYP3A4 has enough signal of its own that sharing a trunk with a noisier label dilutes it.

**The Tier 3 result does not change this choice** (`cypmode.challenge.tier3_features`, run on 2026-09-26 once all 50 structures were complete). The evaluation was restricted to the compounds that Tier 3 covers, because a full-population number would be diluted to near zero by the coverage of 50 of 3,584 compounds whatever the value of the feature. Repeated 5×5 CV with Tukey HSD gave M3 = 0.356 against M3+Tier3 = 0.343 (mean difference -0.0136, p-adj = 0.873, not significant). This is the same null pattern as for the quantum-electronic and pharmacophore-fingerprint features: mechanistically motivated and statistically indistinguishable from no effect. A second reason is more consequential: Tier 3 was selected entirely from training compounds (zero overlap with the 750-row blind test set, confirmed directly), so even a positive result could only have informed the choice of architecture; it could never have become a per-row feature of the deployed model, since the column would be missing for every test-set prediction. The null result and the coverage ceiling point the same way: the model choice for CYP3A4 is the independent-head model with a tuned threshold, and no Tier-3-driven revision follows.

**Reliability score.** The score is cross-cutting and is computed from ensemble disagreement, the Tanimoto distance to the training set and, where Tier 3 ran, the confidence score of Boltz-2. It is kept outside the prediction path. No deliverable calls it "uncertainty quantification" until its correlation with the measured held-out prediction error has been measured and reported.

**Stratified evaluation by chemical familiarity.** In addition to the overall MCC, the validation compounds are split by Tanimoto similarity to the training set (familiar against OOD-like), to test the falsifiable claim that structural and mechanistic augmentation helps most on the compounds that the 2D model is uncertain about. A result of that kind is stronger than an aggregate MCC difference, and it is the basis of the contribution proposed for the Innovation Award.

**Split-validity diagnostic** (`cypmode.challenge.split_validity`). A CV split can look more reliable than it is if the validation compounds are chemically close to the training set (near-duplicates or analogs), which makes it a weaker test of generalization; this is a general risk of cheminformatics benchmarking and is not specific to this challenge. Checked on the data of the project, **the i.i.d. CV folds are chemically less similar to their own training portion (median similarity 0.448, 38.3% at 0.5 or more) than the blinded 750-compound TDI test set is to the full training set (median 0.569, 81.5% at 0.5 or more)**. This is a mildly reassuring sign, not a guarantee, that the CV-measured MCC is unlikely to collapse on the blind set in the way it could if the blind set were the more chemically novel side of the comparison. A mechanism has since been confirmed: the public documentation of the challenge (`config.py`, `ABOUT_MD` of the official HF Space) states that the blind test set was built by "hit expansion... purchasing the top 10 chemisimilars (ECFP4 Tanimoto similarity) per hit compound" from the top hits of the training population (CYP1A2, CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 hits specifically). The similarity finding is therefore a documented consequence of how the test set was constructed. It is reported as a diagnostic with a confirmed mechanism and is not proof of transfer.

  - **The exact TDI label thresholds** (FAQ of the live Space, "How exactly are TDI positive/negative labels assigned?"). Positive: direct-inhibition pIC50 above 4 and shift above 2-fold. Negative: direct-inhibition pIC50 above 4 and shift of 2-fold or less. **Inferred positive: direct-inhibition pIC50 of 4 or less and TDI-arm pIC50 above 4.301** (not simply above 4; 4 + log10(2) = 4.301 is the exact threshold, chosen so that even the worst unmeasured direct-inhibition value of exactly 4 still guarantees a shift above 2-fold). Assigned negative: direct-inhibition pIC50 of 4 or less and TDI-arm pIC50 of 4 or less. This sharpens, and confirms, the diagnosis behind the retraction of the OOF bridge above: the direct-inhibition value of a compound partly defines its own TDI label by an explicit organizer rule and does not only correlate with it.
  - **`evaluation/config.py`**, "ported from the challenge backend, so it should match exactly what you see on the leaderboard": `CLASSIFICATION_ENDPOINTS = ["CYP2D6_is_TDI", "CYP3A4_is_TDI"]`, `CLASSIFICATION_METRICS` = MCC (plain `sklearn.matthews_corrcoef`) with Accuracy, Precision, Recall and F1 as secondary metrics, `SORT_CLASSIFICATION_LEADERBOARD_BY = "MCC"`, `BOOTSTRAP_SAMPLES = 1000` and `ACTIVITY_DATASET_SIZE = 750`.   - **Macro-averaging mechanism.** "MA" (`MACRO_ENDPOINT_LABEL`) is a synthetic pseudo-endpoint computed inside each bootstrap sample (`compute_macro_bootstrap_results`: for every bootstrap sample, the plain arithmetic mean of the per-endpoint metric of CYP3A4 and CYP2D6), and not an average of two independently bootstrapped point estimates. This is functionally close to the assumption made for the reporting of the project, but the mechanism acts per sample, which is what makes the bootstrap confidence intervals of the "MA-MCC" on the leaderboard valid.
  - **The official TDI submission validator** (`validation/tdi_validation.py`) matches the validator of `cypmode.challenge.submission` almost exactly: the same 4 required columns (`SMILES`, `Molecule_Name`, `CYP2D6_is_TDI`, `CYP3A4_is_TDI`), the same 750-row check, and no column-order enforcement (confirmed again, on CYP-specific code this time). This is an assay-level reason why CYP2D6 is the harder isoform for every team on the leaderboard.

**Base-rate-shift-robust thresholding** (`cypmode.challenge.threshold_tuning.find_robust_threshold` and `cross_validate_with_robust_threshold_tuning`). A single MCC-optimal threshold implicitly assumes that the test base rate matches that of the training set; a mismatch can silently cost MCC, a known general risk of any threshold chosen by optimizing on one label distribution and applied to another. The method was implemented as a minimax choice over an assumed base-rate grid (0.05 to 0.50) and validated with a base-rate-shift stress test (resampling the validation-fold labels and probabilities to different rates, not simulated data). **The effect is real and asymmetric.** At the natural (unshifted) rate the robust threshold is close to the naive one (CYP3A4 0.372 against 0.375, CYP2D6 0.080 against 0.078, negligible either way). Under a shift toward a rarer positive class than in the training set (π below about 0.20) the robust threshold helps (for example CYP3A4 at π = 0.05: 0.232 against 0.199 naive). Under a shift toward a more common positive class (π above about 0.30) it hurts, sometimes substantially (CYP3A4 at π = 0.45: 0.415 robust against 0.481 naive): the minimax criterion buys protection in one direction at a cost in the other. A "mean" criterion (the average MCC over the same grid instead of the worst case) was also tested and picked exactly the same threshold as the naive MCC-optimal one in both isoforms, so it gave no improvement. **Decision: not adopted as the default threshold.** There is no evidence that the TDI base rate of the blind test is lower than that of the training set (the split-validity diagnostic above points the other way), so the asymmetric cost of the minimax threshold is not clearly justified. The method is kept as tested and documented infrastructure that can be used if external evidence about the base rate of the blind test appears.

**KNN-label (read-across) features** (`cypmode.challenge.knn_features`). For each compound, the known TDI status of its K nearest chemical neighbors (Tanimoto similarity on ECFP4) is aggregated into 4 features (fraction positive, similarity-weighted fraction positive, mean similarity to those neighbors, neighbor count) and added to M3. The features are built fold-internally and not precomputed globally: the neighbor pool of a training compound excludes the compound itself (leave-one-out) and is restricted to the rest of the training portion of that fold, and the neighbor pool of a validation compound is the full training portion only (never the compound itself and never anything from its own validation fold); the leakage-guard tests in the module docstring explain why a global precompute would leak. **Result with the same 5-fold CV protocol as M3: CYP3A4 MCC 0.300 → 0.312, CYP2D6 MCC 0.043 → 0.075 (+74% relative).** The result is positive for both isoforms and largest in relative terms for CYP2D6, the bottleneck of the project. The features are a candidate for the assembled feature set next to M3.

**Auxiliary direct-CYP-activity features from external data** (`cypmode.challenge.pubchem_auxiliary`, `cypmode.challenge.bindingdb_auxiliary`). One LightGBM classifier per isoform is trained on a broader external compound population and applied to the TDI compounds of the project as an extra feature. No fold-internal OOF treatment is needed, because both sources are fully external to the TDI-labeled data of the project (the module docstrings give the reasoning, which differs from why the OOF Direct-Inhibition Bridge above needs OOF construction).

- **PubChem AID 1851** (NCATS qHTS cytochrome panel, 16,560 compounds after the overlap with the project data is excluded). **Corrected result: CYP3A4 MCC 0.300 → 0.308, CYP2D6 MCC 0.043 → 0.071 (+64% relative).** A first version of the result showed CYP2D6 falling to 0.020; this was traced to a data-handling error and not to a finding. The raw PubChem file has replicate measurement rows per (compound, isozyme) pair (up to 7 for one pair), and merging without collapsing them first multiplied 16,560 compounds into 86,721 rows and over-weighted the compounds that had replicates. The fix is `drop_duplicates` before the merge and not after it. The corrected number is a positive result on both isoforms and not the regression that the error produced. The case is kept on record as a reminder that a surprising ablation result needs its data pipeline checked before it is trusted, the same discipline that is applied to every leakage guard of the project.
- **BindingDB** (literature-curated Ki, IC50 and EC50 values; targeted `getLigandsByUniprots` pulls for CYP3A4/P08684 and CYP2D6/P10635 only, about 3 MB and 2 MB, not the full dumps of 240 MB to 3 GB). Repeated measurements for the same compound are combined by the median affinity; the quirk differs from that of AID 1851 (independent measurements from different papers and not replicate rows of one assay), so median-combining rather than deduplicating is the right choice. **Result: CYP3A4 MCC 0.300 → 0.309, CYP2D6 MCC 0.043 → 0.089 (+107% relative), the largest individual CYP2D6 gain found up to that point**, ahead of the KNN-label features (+74%) and AID 1851 (+64%). A crash occurred and was fixed: a small fraction of the SMILES strings of BindingDB (7 of 8,679 for CYP3A4) carry a ChemAxon extended-annotation suffix that plain RDKit cannot parse. They are dropped and not passed through, after confirming that they are a small fraction of the data (under 0.1%).

All three auxiliary and read-across additions (KNN, AID 1851, BindingDB) were first tested as independent, separate additions to plain M3 at the naive 0.5 threshold, and then combined and statistically tested (see the next section), so that none was left as an isolated ablation, in line with the standing rule that a feature is validated alone and wired into the assembled model.

**Combined-feature stacking and the correction of the significance statements that followed** (`cypmode.challenge.combined_features`, `cypmode.challenge.stacking_significance`). A first attempt at stacking KNN, AID 1851 and BindingDB together for CYP2D6 on a single 5-fold split gave MCC 0.059, worse than any individual addition (0.071 to 0.089). The result was investigated and neither dismissed nor accepted. Dropping AID 1851 (the most redundant of the three, with a correlation of 0.49 with BindingDB) and testing KNN plus BindingDB alone on the same split gave the same pattern (0.054). **The correction was motivated by the methodology of Pat Walters' model-validation-central (patwalters.github.io/model-validation-central, "pooling more methods turns outright wins into ties" without proper correction): the comparison was repeated over 5×5 = 25 folds and Tukey HSD was applied.** **Every pairwise comparison among M3, M3+BindingDB, M3+KNN and M3+KNN+BindingDB was non-significant (all p-adj of 0.52 or more, all confidence intervals crossing zero)**: the fold-to-fold noise (standard deviation of about 0.06 to 0.07) is large relative to the differences between the means (about 0.01 to 0.02) at the sample size of CYP2D6. **The first finding that combining hurts was single-split noise and not a negative interaction.** A surprising ablation result has to survive repeated, corrected testing before it is trusted, the same discipline that applies to the data error found for AID 1851 above. The practical choice is M3+KNN, which has the best point estimate (mean MCC 0.092) and is the simplest of the tied options (one feature source and no external API at inference time), and not because it was shown statistically superior. This mirrors, at a smaller scale, what the organizers reported for the interim TDI leaderboard: 26 teams statistically tied in "Tier 1" by the same kind of corrected significance test (Benjamini-Hochberg), spanning an MCC range of 0.341 to 0.398.

**The same 25-fold procedure was then applied to CYP3A4**, whose only result until then was a single-split combined-feature number (0.300 → 0.321) that looked positive and had not been checked for noise. It does not survive the check: M3 = 0.309 ± 0.055, M3+BindingDB = 0.313 ± 0.055, M3+KNN = 0.321 ± 0.052 and M3+KNN+BindingDB = 0.324 ± 0.045, and every pairwise comparison is again non-significant (all p-adj of 0.74 or more). As for CYP2D6, the single-split 0.321 was within fold-to-fold noise and is not a demonstrated effect. The practical choice for CYP3A4 is of the same kind: M3+KNN or M3+KNN+BindingDB are reasonable defaults on the point estimate, and neither is shown to be statistically superior to plain M3.

**Structure standardization for cross-dataset matching** (`cypmode.challenge.structure_standardization`, through `chembl_structure_pipeline`, a package whose only dependency is RDKit). The exact-overlap leakage checks of both auxiliary modules matched on plain canonical SMILES, which treats a salt form as a different compound from its free base (confirmed: ketoconazole and ketoconazole.Cl canonicalize to two different strings), a gap that could silently miss a duplicate between an external dataset and the compounds of the project. Both modules now match on the standardized parent structure.

**Quantum-derived electronic-structure features** (`cypmode.challenge.quantum_pharmacophore`, through GFN2-xTB of tblite; the module docstring gives the reasons for tblite over xtb-python and for a semi-empirical method over full DFT). The cost was measured: even the crudest DFT-adjacent level, HF/STO-3G through pyscf, takes about 65 seconds on a drug molecule of 71 atoms against about 1 second for tblite, a slowdown of about 65 times that would put the full training set at about 111 hours single-threaded even at that minimal level; pyscf is well maintained and is the wrong tool for a batch of several thousand compounds. The feature vector consists of the HOMO and LUMO energies and gap, the dipole magnitude, the total energy and summary statistics of the partial charges. The single-split result was mixed or negative for both isoforms (CYP3A4 0.312 → 0.308, CYP2D6 0.043 → 0.028) and, in line with the standing rule, was checked with the same repeated 5×5 CV and Tukey HSD. **The negative reading does not survive the check**: for both isoforms every pairwise comparison among M3, M3+KNN, M3+Quantum and M3+KNN+Quantum is non-significant (CYP3A4 all p-adj of 0.85 or more; CYP2D6 all p-adj of 0.16 or more). The quantum feature is statistically indistinguishable from plain M3 in either direction: it is not shown to be harmful or helpful. This is consistent with every other single-split result that looked interesting (KNN plus BindingDB for both isoforms): a single 5-fold split is not enough to trust, whichever direction it points.

**3-point pharmacophore fingerprints** (`cypmode.challenge.pharmacophore_fingerprint`, through pmapper, an actively maintained package with a light RDKit and NetworkX dependency and no compiled numerical backend, so that it carries no OpenMP-conflict risk with LightGBM of the kind that tblite has). The fingerprint encodes the 3D spatial arrangement of pharmacophoric features (hydrogen-bond donors and acceptors, aromatic rings, hydrophobic regions, ionizable groups) relative to each other as a 2048-bit fingerprint. It is a distinct feature family from the 2D Morgan fingerprint already in M3 and from the 0D scalar quantum-electronic features, since neither captures the 3D arrangement of features. The cost is about 350 ms per compound (about 36 minutes single-threaded for the full training set). The 25-fold repeated CV with Tukey HSD was run directly and not after a single split, following the lesson of the quantum feature. **The result is the same as for the quantum feature**: no pairwise comparison is significant for either isoform (CYP3A4 all p-adj of 0.56 or more; CYP2D6 all p-adj of 0.15 or more), and the point estimate leans slightly negative for both (CYP3A4 M3+Pharmacophore = 0.303 against M3 = 0.309; CYP2D6 0.053 against 0.067), a harm that is not significant either; there is no evidence of benefit.

**A pattern across three independent feature additions** (KNN plus BindingDB, quantum-electronic, pharmacophore fingerprint): none moves the MCC of M3 by a statistically defensible amount for either isoform, although each is mechanistically motivated. Three external signals point in the same direction: the finding of the OpenADMET-ExpansionRx retrospective that classical machine learning (LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost) ranked no higher than 46th among the top ADMET-challenge submissions, and two published CYP-specific papers (the FP-GNN of idrugLab and the MTMM-CYP of Seomun and Yoo) that are both built around the fusion of several learned representations (fingerprint and GNN, or graph, convolution and transformer) and not around classical descriptors alone. The evidence taken together supports the reading that the ceiling is the classical-descriptor-plus-LightGBM architecture of M3 itself and not a missing hand-crafted feature. This motivates the ChemBERTa work below: a different representation (learned, pretrained, transformer-based) and not another hand-engineered one.

**Transformer-based SMILES embeddings** (`cypmode.challenge.chemberta_features`, through ChemBERTa, `seyonec/ChemBERTa-zinc-base-v1`, a published RoBERTa model pretrained on 100k ZINC SMILES strings, as its model card states). The embeddings are a third molecular representation next to the two of M3, following the methodological idea of MTMM-CYP (several representations, shared across isoforms) without adopting its code (pinned to Python 3.7 and TensorFlow 1.x, no licence; see the Decision Log). Confirmed: a 768-dimensional mean-pooled embedding at about 35 ms per molecule on a CPU (about 3.6 minutes for the full training set), with no 3D conformer step (the model reads the tokenized SMILES), so there is essentially no embedding-failure mode of the kind that the quantum and pharmacophore features have. **Architectural constraint, confirmed**: `import torch` followed by fitting an `LGBMClassifier` in the same process segfaults (exit code 139), the same OpenMP conflict that is documented for `cypmode.challenge.multitask`, and it extends to this module. The `_main()` of `chemberta_features.py` therefore only extracts and caches embeddings, and the evaluation against M3 runs in a separate process (`cypmode.challenge.chemberta_significance`, LightGBM only, never importing torch), the same isolation that `tests/test_multitask.py` requires.

**Adding the raw ChemBERTa embeddings to the feature vector of M3 lowers the CYP3A4 MCC, but the loss is not statistically established.** M3 = 0.309 → M3+ChemBERTa = 0.255 (mean difference -0.0545, paired corrected t with Holm adjustment: adjusted p = 0.23, 95% interval -0.111 to +0.002), and M3+KNN = 0.321 → M3+KNN+ChemBERTa = 0.256 (mean difference -0.0644, adjusted p = 0.11). An earlier version of this section reported these as significant (Tukey HSD, adjusted p = 0.0002 and below 0.0001); that test treated the 25 correlated folds of each variant as independent groups and is replaced by the paired comparison (`stacking_significance.paired_comparison_summary`, applied to the saved per-fold scores by `recompute_paired_summaries.py`). On CYP2D6 no comparison is significant (smallest adjusted p = 0.82). Under both tests the point estimates move in the same direction, and the addition was not adopted.

**Interpretation: a data-representation mismatch, not evidence against transformer embeddings in general.** Concatenating a dense, highly correlated 768-dimensional embedding onto the already large feature vector of M3 and passing the result to a tree-based gradient-boosting model trained on only about 3,500 to 4,800 examples is a recipe for the model spending its limited splits on noisy embedding dimensions instead of the cleaner signal in the fingerprint and descriptors; LightGBM has no learned projection step that compresses or denoises a dense embedding as a neural fusion layer would. This is consistent with the MTMM-CYP, FP-GNN and ExpansionRx pattern noted above: those approaches do not append a transformer embedding to the raw feature matrix of a tree model either, and route each representation through its own learned branch before the fusion.

**A neural fusion architecture was therefore built and tested** (`MultiBranchSharedTrunkModel` in `cypmode.challenge.multitask`: M3 and the ChemBERTa embedding each have their own branch (Linear, ReLU, Dropout) before the concatenation into the shared trunk, in place of one raw block). **It does not help either**: on the same single 5-fold split it is slightly worse than the M3-only shared trunk. CYP3A4: shared trunk with M3 only 0.238 (untuned) and 0.266 (threshold-tuned) against 0.227 for the shared trunk with M3 and ChemBERTa fusion. CYP2D6: 0.099 (untuned) and 0.113 (tuned) against 0.092. Two independent attempts to use ChemBERTa point in the same direction: naive LightGBM concatenation lowers the score (the loss is not significant under the paired corrected t), and a neural fusion architecture shows no benefit (not yet checked at 25-fold rigor, but the point estimate moved the wrong way both times). **Conclusion for this dataset at this size (about 4,800 compounds, at most about 6,100)**: a pretrained general-purpose SMILES transformer embedding does not appear to add signal beyond the descriptor and fingerprint representation of M3 for CYP TDI classification. The outcome differs from ADMET regression tasks at larger scale (ExpansionRx: about 7,600 compounds, continuous multi-endpoint), where GNN and foundation-model approaches won, and it is a finding specific to this task and not a general statement about transformer embeddings.

The M3-only shared-trunk neural model (0.25 to 0.27 for CYP3A4) does not beat the classical LightGBM M3 baseline (about 0.31) on this task even without ChemBERTa, so the shared-trunk hypothesis remains unproven independently of the ChemBERTa question.

## Corrected TDI population and selection-corrected evaluation (2026-10-04)

**This section supersedes the CYP3A4 CV numbers and the conclusions above that no feature helps, wherever they conflict.** Three facts, each verified against the training file and the public boards, were established.

1. **The label is a deterministic function of two columns that had not been used.** `positive <=> t - max(d, 4) > log10(2)` (t = pIC50 of the TDI arm, d = direct-inhibition pIC50, floor 4) reproduces every given label exactly (`cypmode.challenge.tdi_targets`, tested against the training file).
2. **A third of the labeled CYP3A4 rows follow a different convention from that of the scored test set.** 1,249 of 3,584 rows have no direct pIC50 (but a potent TDI arm, median 5.4) and are all labeled negative; the blinded test set was assayed in both arms, so it has no such rows. Training on them and evaluating on them, as every earlier CYP3A4 number did, mixes two populations. On the 2,334 rows with both arms the same LightGBM gains +0.033 AUC (0.724 → 0.757) from dropping them. The earlier feature ablations (KNN, BindingDB, AID1851, ChemBERTa and others) were run on the mixed population and were repeated on the corrected one. CYP2D6 is unaffected (1,493 of 1,497 rows have both arms).
3. **Random CV does not resemble the test.** The test compounds are analogs of top screening hits: 54% have a training neighbor at Tanimoto 0.5 or more, against 33% in a CV fold, and the official baseline scores about 0.08 to 0.13 MCC higher on the boards than in the CV of the project. The positive rates differ from the training set as well (CYP3A4 about 0.31 and CYP2D6 about 0.059 against 0.213 and 0.216), and were recovered from the leaderboard rows by `cypmode.challenge.leaderboard_prior` and not typed in. `cypmode.challenge.validation_matching` re-weights the validation predictions to the nearest-neighbor-similarity profile and the prior of the test set; it narrows the gap and does not close it, so only the order of the candidates under matched conditions is meaningful.

**Selection is part of what is evaluated.** `cypmode.challenge.nested` fixes a plan of outer and inner folds on disk (the inner folds lie inside the outer-training rows), lets any model in any environment fill a prediction cache from it, and scores the whole procedure on outer rows that it never saw: the choice of candidate, the blend weights (an L1 or L2 stack with the penalty chosen in the inner loop) and the operating threshold (bootstrap-smoothed and transferred as a positive rate and not as a score). No number in it is set by hand: the prior, the similarity matching, the thresholds and the stack weights are all derived from data. The tests include a pure-noise case (the best of 40 noise models: inner AUC above 0.58, outer about 0.50) and leakage guards for every runner. First numbers (similarity- and prior-matched, 5 outer folds): CYP3A4 best single candidate AUC 0.771, stack 0.782 (MCC about 0.38); CYP2D6 best single 0.683, stack 0.684 (MCC about 0.11 to 0.13); the selection optimism is about 0 or negative. Extra fingerprint blocks add 0.01 AUC or less, and no block helps CYP2D6.

**Guarded in addition.** Training-fold-only standardization has a regression test (`tests/test_multitask_no_standardization_leak.py`, mutation-checked). Two label-free feature blocks were added to the store: `ext_activity` (inhibitor probabilities from models trained only on the Veith panels, with any challenge compound removed from the external training set) and `tdi_alerts` (structural alerts of mechanism-based inactivation).

## Uncertainty: query by committee, tested against the measured errors (2026-10-05)

**Question.** Query by committee (QBC) measures uncertainty as the disagreement inside a diverse ensemble of distinct models, in place of the stochastic passes of a single model (MC dropout) or the seed-to-seed spread of one algorithm (what `cypmode.challenge.reliability.ensemble_disagreement` did, with a correlation of about 0.10 with the error). The nested harness already holds out-of-fold predictions for 25 heterogeneous candidates, so the committee needs no further training. `cypmode.challenge.committee_uncertainty` evaluates it on compounds that no candidate or selector saw (all nested outer folds: 2,334 for CYP3A4 and 1,493 for CYP2D6) against a bar higher than correlation with the error: the signal must add information beyond the **margin** (the distance of the score of the stack from its own decision threshold), judged against a permutation null with Bonferroni correction, and acting on it (falling back to a negative call on the most uncertain compounds) must improve MCC.

**Result.** AUROC for flagging the mistakes of the stack, margin against committee (vote entropy and spread): CYP3A4 0.687 against 0.620 and 0.557, CYP2D6 0.599 against 0.593 and 0.457. For CYP3A4 every committee signal is redundant with the margin (gain over the margin -0.001, p of 0.2 or more). For CYP2D6, vote entropy and spread each add a small but statistically detectable amount beyond the margin (+0.009 and +0.016 AUROC, permutation p = 0.005; the relationship of the spread is inverted, with high disagreement meaning fewer errors), yet acting on them lowers MCC (0.096 → 0.063 to 0.087 for entropy), so neither may alter a prediction. Restricting the committee to its best members, or to one algorithm family, did not help, and diversity across families did not beat a LightGBM-only committee on CYP3A4. The mistakes are mostly collective (all models miss the same information), and a committee can see only the mistakes about which the models disagree.

**Decision.** The committee signals and the margin are part of the reliability table (`reliability.compute_reliability_score`, deployment values in `data/tdi_track/outputs/nested/<isoform>/reliability_deploy.csv`) and are reported and not used: a component alters a prediction only if it adds information beyond the margin and acting on it improves MCC (`decision_record` writes this per component to `data/tdi_track/outputs/comparisons/committee_uncertainty_decision_<isoform>.json`). At present no component does. The result is a measured negative result, kept as such on purpose: an accuracy gain is reproducible, and an uncertainty claim that was never checked against the simplest baseline is not.

## Update: nested evaluation, gated additions and the assembled model

The deployed design described above (m3_no_amine_features with independent LightGBM heads for CYP3A4 and a shared trunk for CYP2D6, tuned
thresholds) predates the nested evaluation harness. The current TDI engine is documented in the README (architecture sketches for both
tracks), in `METHOD_REPORT.md` and `NEGATIVE_RESULTS.md` (generated from the run logs by `cypmode/audit/method_report.py`) and in
`audit/AUDIT_REPORT.md`. In short:

- Evaluation is nested cross-validation (repeats x outer x inner folds) on the population the challenge scores: both arms measured,
  similarity to the test structures, estimated test prevalence and confident labels at two cut-offs (z0 = 1.0 and 1.96).
- The model is a logistic stack of the members of its isoform (boosted trees on several feature sets, forests, Tanimoto kNN/SVM, two-arm regressors,
  and the groups that passed their gates; the counts are in the tables at the end), calibrated per isoform, called by a pre-declared multiple of the scored prevalence.
- Every addition (physics blocks, SAR pair and matched-pair models, label-process committee, frozen embeddings, test-likeness weighting,
  floor-clipped arms, selection-aware CYP2D6 model) is a candidate group that enters the stack only when its pre-registered gate is met:
  corrected resampled t below 0.05 at both cut-offs, alone and inside the stack. `cypmode/challenge/final_tdi_build.py` applies the gates.
- The structure track uses Boltz-2 (optionally OpenFold3) with levers that each pass a gate on the crystal-anchored benchmark;
  `cypmode/validation/final_recipe.py` maps the verdicts to the recipe. The benchmark is ten CYP3A4 co-crystal ligands of the PDB picked by a rule fixed in advance
  (`cypmode/validation/structure_benchmark.py`: 20 to 60 heavy atoms, resolution at most 3.0 A, organic, one fragment, then the most mutually dissimilar by a maximin pick
  on Morgan fingerprints); the method report (section 11) lists them with their PDB entries.

Significance statements in the sections above that rest on Tukey's HSD were recomputed from their saved per-fold scores with the paired
corrected resampled t (Holm-adjusted); where the two differ the paired result is the one stated.

Additions to the engine since that section was written, each behind its own gate:

- Physics blocks (GFN2-xTB through tblite, in a separate process): tier A (133 columns: molecule, atom, site-role and pharmacophore
  geometry), a compact variant that keeps the 20 columns with the largest gain chosen from the training rows of each task
  (`lgbm_clf_select`), tier B (Boltzmann-weighted conformers and locally relaxed hydrogen abstraction), and the same block inside the
  selection-aware CYP2D6 model (`population_physics.py`).
- Replicate design with 10 outer folds (`replicate_verdict.py`, `final_tdi_build.py --design 10`) for the physics additions; the variance
  factor of the corrected resampled t drops from 0.317 to 0.144.
- Hedged blend of the hits-only and selection-aware CYP2D6 stacks (`hedged_blend.py`) and a label-shift estimate of the never-promoted
  share of the test structures (`test_nonhit_share.py`); the blend is written as a candidate only if its minimax gate is met.
- Choice of the final CYP2D6 file (`selection_decision.py`, job 016, a procedure fixed before any upload): at the call rate of the final build the selection-aware file gains little where it wins and loses more where the scored set holds no never-promoted compound, and the live board, which scores half of the test set, cannot separate the two files, so the entry is the hits-only file by the registered rule. This is the working of exploration against exploitation in this project: every idea is tried in the open with its gate, the entry exploits only what the registered rule supports, and the idea that the rule left out is kept to be tested rather than dropped.
- Structure track: pose terms that use the ligand and pocket physics (`pose_awareness.py`: match to the xTB conformer ensemble, burial,
  contacts, clashes, Fe-donor error, oxidation-site window), SAR consistency across analogs (`series.py`, `series_consensus.py`), the

- Tooling: `cypmode/audit/checklist.py` (`--status`), `cypmode/audit/public_readiness.py`, `scripts/reproduce_tdi.sh`,
  `python -m notebooks.build_notebook`, and `cypmode/audit/project_map.py`, which writes the four pages of `docs/map` (the code, the queue, the flow of the data, the evidence)
  from the tracked files; the pages are checked by a test.
- Cryo-EM protein templates (`cryoem_templates.py`): the five cryo-EM CYP3A4 entries 38FU-38FY (chain A of each trimer) as one more pose-diversity lever, judged by the same pilot gate as the crystal-conformer, seed and step-scale levers.

- Second looks at the assembled stack (`all_in_one.py`, `atomjepa_head`): the stack of every cached candidate whose predictions are complete, compared with the final stack of each isoform on the same plan (corrected paired t at both cut-offs and the design floor); it is registered not to replace the final stack by itself, because it has no replicate. The Atom-JEPA readout is repeated on 10 outer folds. The verdicts are in the `exploratory_*` logs, which no gate parser reads.

A screen is not a gate and is not in the ledger. CYP2D6 is not part of this design (decision of 2026-10-16, confirmed on 2026-10-22): plain Chemprop is at chance on it (outer AUC 0.54 and 0.53) and the full design costs about 12 days of machine time for an expected gain below the smallest difference of about 0.01 that the design can certify, so that the idea was not tried there; whether the OpenADMET pretraining weights help on CYP2D6 has not been tested (limitation L12 of the method report).

A feasibility rule written before the timing probe (job 259: at most 48 hours of exclusive machine time for the 10 benchmark and the 20 official ligands with 3 samples each) allowed a benchmark of the 10 crystal ligands (job 260) and the engine-consensus gate of the structure track with BioEmu2 as the added engine (job 261); official poses are generated only if that gate is met.
- Pocket-level pharmacophore block (`cypmode/challenge/pocket_pharmacophore.py`, declared before any result): the pockets of CYP3A4 and CYP2D6 as density fields of the feature families of their public crystal ligands in the frame of the heme, and for every compound the best placement of up to 12 conformers by a donor or a substrate anchor and 1080 orientations, as 22 columns per pocket; added to the deep boosted-tree candidates behind the usual gate and a replicate, with the pocket of the other enzyme as a control that enters no gate.
- Two pharmacophore models (`cypmode/challenge/pocket_pharmacophore.py`, `cypmode/challenge/pharmacophore_hypotheses.py`, declared before any code or result): a structure-based site model per enzyme (the local maxima of the six feature fields of the crystal ligands as weighted sites with a tolerance of 1.5 A, and for every placed compound the recall, the number of matched sites and the precision, six columns per pocket) and a ligand-based hypothesis model (two- and three-feature pmapper signatures realised by the conformers of a compound, the 60 most enriched among the positives and the 60 among the negatives of a training split, chosen by a chi-square statistic and weighted by a Laplace-corrected log-likelihood ratio, five columns that the training rows receive out of fold). Each is added to the deep boosted-tree candidates behind the usual gate and a 10-fold replicate; the site model has the site model of the other enzyme as its control, the hypothesis model a permuted-label control, and neither control enters a gate.
- Pharmacophore model of the predicted poses (`cypmode/validation/pose_pharmacophore.py`, declared on 2026-10-23 before any code): a pose is put in the frame of its own heme and scored against the fields and the sites of the crystal ligands rebuilt without the scored ligand and its analogues (the gate is the bar of the pose terms), and the expected pharmacophore of the 20 official ligands is described from the final poses and their pools (`official_pharmacophore.py`, held back). Two checks of what such a model cannot see are registered with it: the conformational bias (`conformer_bias.py`: how often the ensemble of low-energy conformers contains the bound conformation of 148 crystal ligand copies) and the binding-site representation (`site_representation.py`: the variability of the crystal pocket, the distance of a predicted pocket and of a pose from what the crystals show). The ensembles of conformers are drawn with the seed 42; the seed 0 returns the same conformer every time in the installed RDKit.

The scorer's PoseBusters call (the `dock` configuration) has no chirality check, so a pose of the wrong enantiomer passes it with the same margin; the stereochemistry of a pose is read from its coordinates and a pose is eligible for the submission only when it carries the official one (`stereo_ok` of `pose_margins`, used by `final_recipe`, checked on the submitted zip by the audit and the pre-submission gate). The runs made from the old SMILES are set aside unchanged, every run of the three ligands is made again from the corrected SMILES, the recipe chooses again under the unchanged rule, and the final chain runs again.
